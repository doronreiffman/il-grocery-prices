# The shopper site: design

**Status:** draft of 2026-11-06, for the owner's approval before any code. It follows the owner's decision of 2026-11-05: the public site is built around three per-product features, "Is this deal real?", "Wait for the sale?" and "Real deals this week", and the daily headline basket no longer leads. Every choice the owner has not made is marked "Call" and listed at the end; the owner can overturn any. It needs the promotion reader (the project's AI piece, designed separately) and says what the site needs from it; it does not design the reader.
**Grade:** portfolio grade, as the headline basket ([headline-basket.md](headline-basket.md)): tested, reproducible, honest in its words; not production. Two parts are held to a record standard: each day's frozen forecasts and their later scores, and the published accuracy tables, are write-once and change only the way a published basket day changes (rule B12: with a dated reason, the earlier figures kept).

## What this piece is, and why

The collector keeps, every morning, the price files and promotion files five Israeli chains publish under the law ([collector.md](collector.md)). The headline basket turned one morning's price files into one number. This site turns the whole archive into something a shopper acts on: for one product at one chain, whether today's promotion is a price the item has not been listed at lately or one it is listed at most weeks anyway; how often the item goes on promotion and how long since the last one; and, once the site has earned it, the odds of a promotion in the next two weeks, with the site's own record of how right those odds have been. The portfolio point is the same as the basket's, carried further: data engineering on messy public data, with claims no stronger than the evidence, and a forecast whose accuracy is published before anyone is asked to trust it. The owner's flight-board site leads the same way, with a traveller's own flight and the odds from here, scored.

Two readers: a shopper deciding today, who finds one product and reads one page; and a hiring reader skimming the portfolio, who should see in half a minute what the site does, that its claims are measured, and who built it.

Others already compare current prices across chains (CHP, Pricez, Salai, Smoody) and that is fine: novelty is not a filter (owner, 2026-11-05). None of them, as far as the project's research of 2026-10-31 found, publishes how a promotion price compares with the item's own listed history, or a scored forecast. That is a search result, not proof.

## What a visitor sees

One site in English with Hebrew product names, as the basket page is ([headline-basket.md](headline-basket.md), call 2); a Hebrew version is a later step (Call 12, and a question for the owner: the shopper reads Hebrew, the hiring reader English).

1. **The front page** (`index.html`): "Deals below the usual listed price today" (the public title of the owner's "Real deals this week"; Call 1, the words below). Under the site's name, the date line: "from the chains' price and promotion files of Thursday 6 November 2026". Then: a count line ("Today the five chains list 1,840 promotions on 6,210 products; 1,120 of them are priced below the product's own listed price here over the past 30 days"; figures illustrative); a search box (a barcode or a product name; the no-script path is the browse-by-letter pages); the browse table of today's deals sorted by depth, filterable by chain, club-only and minimum spend (and, once the reader supplies a category, by category); one deal's story as a paragraph in the product page's words; the line for the hiring reader ("Built by …, How this is made, Track record, Code on GitHub"); the credit line.
2. **A product page** (`p/<barcode>.html`), one per product the site covers (rule P1): the names each chain lists, as published ("Grissini classic 200 g (as Shufersal lists it)"), the barcode and the size the chains state; then one section per chain that lists it: today's listed price and the range across its stores; today's promotion, if any, with its price, its conditions and where each came from (the chain's structured fields, the reader, or "fine print not read"); the **"Is this deal real?"** block: the gap between the promotion price and the item's listed price at that chain over the past 30 and 90 days, in the sentences of rule P7; the **"Wait for the sale?"** block: how many of the days with records the item was on promotion here, the last promotion's dates, the gaps between promotions seen, and, once the gate is open (F8), the odds of a promotion within 14 days with the record's one-line summary; a listed-price history as text and a small inline chart (the days on promotion marked). Then a row across chains: today's listed price at each chain, with no ranking words.
3. **Browse-by-letter pages** (`browse/<letter>.html`): every covered product by the first letter of its longest published name, plus one page for codes with no Hebrew name; the path to every product without a script, and the search index's source.
4. **"How this is made"** (`method.html`): the rules below in plain words, the words the site never uses, what is measured and what is inferred, the law, how anyone with the chains' files reproduces a page.
5. **"Track record"** (`record.html`): the forecast scorecard (F10) from the first scored day, baselines beside it, gate open or closed and why; the reader's accuracy per field against the hand-checked set, with the set's size and date; the days the site has, and the days a chain's files were short.
6. **The basket page** (`basket.html`): the headline basket as it is today, with each of its 32 items linked to its product page; its day files go on accruing (Call 10).
7. **Data files**: `days/<day>.json` (the day's counts and every number on the front page, as the basket's day file does), `forecasts/<day>.csv.gz` (the day's frozen forecasts, F5), `search/<shard>.json` (the search index, in pieces).

Every page: the header band with the site's name, the date line, `noindex` until the owner launches, the credit line in the footer ("Prices and promotions from the price and promotion files Shufersal, Rami Levy, Osher Ad, Carrefour and Yochananof publish under section 30 of the Food and Pharma Competition Promotion Law, 2014: the files of 6 November 2026, collected by 12:03 Israel time. Listed prices and listed promotions, not a promise of what a till charges. Not shopping advice."), the look of the basket page (one style block, system fonts, nothing loaded from elsewhere, dark mode, phones).

## Goals

- A shopper answers "take this deal, or wait?" for one product at one chain in under a minute, from measured history and plainly labelled inferences.
- Every number reproducible: the rules published, each day's inputs named in the day file, the same code on the same files giving the same page.
- A forecast published only after it has beaten two rules of thumb on frozen, later-scored forecasts, with the record public from the first scored day, gate open or not.
- A fresh site every morning without the owner, from the files the collector stored that day; the pages say which day they show.
- Cheap: about $1 to $3 a month on top of the collector in year one (the cost section).

## Non-goals (version one)

- Prices or promotions by store, sub-chain or city: chain level, with the store range shown, as the basket does (Call 5; Shufersal's formats price differently, below).
- Promotions that start and end within a day, or appear only in update files: not seen until the update files are collected through the day (a separate design); the site says "listed in the morning's files".
- Coupons, gift items and meal-card vouchers: counted, never shown as deals (P3).
- Delivered prices, online stores, unit-price comparisons across different sizes, brand or private-label analysis, a food index.
- Product matching by name: a product is a barcode; produce and other internal codes get no page.
- A server, a database behind the pages, accounts, alerts to shoppers.
- Airflow and dbt: the build is a handful of steps in one scheduled function, the rules SQL over Parquet, as the basket's are; an orchestrator arrives when the step count earns it (the project's 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-02 decisions).

## Anti-goals (a failure even if everything else works)

- A claim the files cannot back: a motive ("the chain inflates its prices before a sale"), a till price, a saving "for you", a store named as dear or cheap. The site measures gaps and reports them.
- The word "fake", or any word that says a chain deceives. The Consumer Protection Authority's spokesman warned that manipulative use of the data invites misleading-claims suits (Globes via N12, 2025-12-16, a secondary source). The measured gap, in the chain's own listed prices, is the whole statement.
- Odds shown before the gate opens, or a record that resets quietly when the model changes. Every frozen forecast names its model version; the scorecard shows every version's days.
- A page that looks current but is not: the date in every header, the morning dead-man check, and a build that refuses to publish a day older than the site's latest.
- A number nobody can reproduce, or one that changes quietly: B12's rule holds for day files, scores and accuracy tables alike.
- Raw files or per-store price lists published; anything that touches the portals (the build reads the archive only).
- A page whose measured facts need a script: scripts enhance (search, the odds line), and the HTML carries every measured number.

## Definitions and rules

Each rule has a name, tests of its own and a mutation in `scripts/mutation_check.py`. Chain-level price rules reuse the basket's where they apply (B1 files, B2 included stores, B3 rows, B4 placeholders, B5 the chain's median).

- **P1 A product.** A barcode that passes the GS1 check digit, leading zeros stripped, as the basket defines it (headline-basket.md, "The basket"); internal codes (weighed produce, chain codes) are not products here. The site covers a product when, on some day of the past 90 days, at least half of some chain's included stores (B2) listed it (B6's "chain-wide" test); within one chain a product is (chain, barcode), across chains the same barcode. Measured today on the archive's five tables of 2026-11-02 (every store with a file counted, the online stores and Shufersal's Be stores not yet excluded, so Shufersal's chain-wide count is low): 70,698 distinct GS1 barcodes priced above ₪0.01 at any store (Shufersal 38,064; Rami Levy 25,879; Carrefour 20,768; Yochananof 16,902; Osher Ad 9,659); 21,324 listed by at least half of some chain's stores; 8,487 of those at two or more chains; 1,120 at all five; 2,467 at any store of all five (headline-basket.md). So about 21,000 product pages on one day, more over 90 days (Call 3).
- **P2 Listed price.** A chain's listed price for a product on a day is rule B5: the median of the product's price across the chain's included stores whose latest full price file that day lists it, placeholders dropped (B4); the lowest and highest store prices are kept and shown. A chain-day with files from fewer than half its included stores (B8) is a hole: no listed price that day, never a zero. Shufersal's formats price differently (one Sheli store was dearer than one Deal store on 89% of shared items, median 7.7%, measured on two stores' files of 2026-10-31, kept in the project's research notes outside this repository), so the chain's median blurs them; the range beside every price shows the spread, and a sub-chain cut is a later query over the same tables (Call 5).
- **P3 A promotion.** A `<Promotion>` in a chain's full promotion files (`PromoFull`) of the day, linked to the product's barcode through its groups' items, whose start is on or before the day and whose end is on or after it. Excluded and counted: coupons (`AdditionalIsCoupon` = 1), gift items (`IsGiftItem` set), and any promotion whose fields and text give no price effect (a `RewardType` 0 link with no `DiscountedPrice` or `DiscountRate`). In one Shufersal store's file of 2026-10-31, 116 of 258 promotions were coupons, two meal-card vouchers among them carrying about three quarters of all item links; the 142 others were shelf deals (the research sample above). A promotion is **listed chain-wide** when the files of at least half the chain's stores with a file that day carry it; one carried by fewer stores is counted, shown as "at some stores" and never ranked. The fields' meanings (`RewardType` 2 percent off, 3 fixed price, 1 and 10 "N for ₪M" with `MinQty` N and `DiscountedPrice` M; `MinPurchaseAmount` on a second group for "only with a ₪X basket") were read from Shufersal's data, not from a specification, and were checked on Shufersal alone: UNVERIFIED for the other four chains until the first extraction compares them (the build stops loudly on a field set it does not know: P10).
- **P4 Deal price.** The price per unit after the promotion, when buying the least that qualifies: a fixed price is `DiscountedPrice`; percent off is the listed price × (1 − `DiscountRate`/100), with `DiscountedPrice` as a check; "N for ₪M" is M ÷ N, shown with "buy N" as a condition. The fields validate when they agree with each other and the deal price is above ₪0.01 and below twice the listed price; 1,478 of 1,482 non-coupon item links agreed with the shelf price in the sample. A promotion whose fields do not validate takes the reader's reading (R1) when it has one, else it is **unread**: listed with "price not read", never ranked. Weighed goods are the known trap: the sample's three disagreements were all meat ("2 for ₪100" stored as 1.5 for ₪75, the same per-kilogram rate), so a deal price per kilogram is shown per kilogram.
- **P5 Conditions.** Club-only: the promotion's target field says club (the regulations' schedule 3 codes, as read from the Hebrew Wikisource copy of the price-transparency regulations on 2026-10-31: 0 none, 1 club, 2 the chain's credit card, 3 other; the official text is unread: UNVERIFIED) or the reader found a club or card restriction in the text (Shufersal's field read "all customers" on every one of the 258, and its "תו זהב" and meal-card restrictions appeared only in the text). Minimum spend: `MinPurchaseAmount` on a group, or the reader's reading of "מותנה N" and its kin. Also, from the reader: a quantity cap, a day or hour restriction, "while stocks last". Each condition shown names its source: "(from the chain's fields)", "(read from the fine print)". A promotion with no reading yet carries "fine print not read: club-only or other conditions may apply" (step 1 below).
- **P6 Windows.** The past 30 and 90 days are calendar days ending the day before the page's day, counting only days with a listed price at that chain (holes skipped, never filled). A window with fewer than 20 of 30 or 60 of 90 days of records says so ("23 days of records since 1 November 2026"); until 30 days exist the page says "since 1 November 2026", and every count is "of the N days with records".
- **P7 The gap ("Is this deal real?").** Three measured facts, in this order, each a sentence: the deal price against the 30-day median listed price (the reference for depth: Call 4), as money and a whole percent ("₪4.00, 34%, below the 30-day median listed price of ₪11.90"); the deal price against the lowest listed price of the past 30 and 90 days, and on how many of those days the item was listed at or below the deal price without a promotion ("listed at ₪7.90 or less on 0 of the past 90 days"); and any change in the listed price in the past 30 days ("the listed price rose from ₪10.90 to ₪11.90 on 28 October"). Depth is (30-day median listed price − deal price) ÷ that median. No verdict word: the reader of the page draws the conclusion.
- **P8 Promotion history ("Wait for the sale?", the measured part).** On how many of the days with records the product was on a chain-wide promotion at that chain; each promotion seen as a date range (consecutive listed days; a hole inside a run does not break it); the gaps between runs, in days; the last run's end and the days since. All from the morning files (a promotion listed for part of a day is unseen until the update files are collected).
- **P9 Across chains.** One row: each chain's listed price today and its deal price today if any, with the conditions' presence marked; sorted by chain name, never by price; no "cheaper", no rank.
- **P10 Fail loud on a format change.** A promotion file whose field set differs from the one the fixtures hold, or a `RewardType` or target code outside the set seen, stops that chain's extraction with an error naming the field, and the day's build goes on without that chain's promotions and says so on the page ("Promotions from Carrefour's files could not be read today"). The Authority is rewriting the promotion format with a uniform definition of promotions (Globes via N12, 2025-12-16, secondary); the first sign of it must be an email, not a quiet zero.

**Measured or inferred.** Measured, from the files by rule: listed prices and ranges, promotion presence and dates, deal prices where the fields validate, field-based conditions, the gaps and counts of P7 and P8. Computed by rule and named as such: the chain's median, chain-wide coverage, depth. Inferred, and labelled on the page: the reader's readings (R1), with their confidence; the forecast (F). Nothing on a page mixes the two without saying which is which.

## The forecast ("Wait for the sale?", the odds)

A careful forecaster freezes the forecast before the outcome, scores it when the outcome is known, compares it with rules of thumb anyone could have used, and publishes the odds only once the record shows they beat those rules. The rules:

- **F1 Target.** For a product at a chain on day d: 1 if a chain-wide promotion (P3) on it is listed on at least one of the days d+1 to d+14, else 0. The outcome is known on the morning of d+15.
- **F2 Who gets a forecast.** Every covered (chain, product) that the chain lists chain-wide on day d and that is not on a chain-wide promotion on day d (an item on promotion today has no "wait" question). About 39,000 (chain, product) pairs were chain-wide on 2026-11-02 by the measurement in P1, an upper bound on a day's forecasts.
- **F3 Model family, version 1: simple first.** A pooled hazard with shrinkage: for each chain, the share of past forecast-days with outcome 1 among pairs with the same number of days since their last promotion run ended (0 to 60 days, and "no promotion seen"), blended with the pair's own past outcome rate by weight n ÷ (n + n0), n the pair's scored days and n0 one constant fixed in advance. Two ingredients, one constant. Anything more (weekday and holiday effects, a category from the reader, the listed price's recent change, the chain's promotion calendar) joins only as the flight-board site's decision of 2026-11-05 has it: named in advance, its lift measured with an interval on days before a cutoff, the model's score with and without it, then re-tested on later days; never added because it looked good on the days it was fitted to.
- **F4 Two baselines.** (A) **The item's base rate:** the pair's share of past forecast-days with outcome 1 over the past 90 days; with fewer than 20 scored days, the chain's share instead. (B) **"The last gap repeats":** with two or more promotion runs seen, the next run starts at the last run's end plus the gap between the last two runs; the forecast is 1 if that date falls in d+1 to d+14, else 0; with fewer than two runs, 0. B is a yes-or-no rule, so its Brier score is its error rate; that is the point: a reader can apply B by eye, and the model must beat it.
- **F5 The daily freeze.** The build writes the day's forecasts once, write-once and locked like a manifest (`If-None-Match: *`, the ten-year governance lock), to `forecasts/date=<day>/forecasts-<run id>.parquet`: chain, barcode, the model's probability, both baselines, the features used (days since the last run, n), the model version and its constants, the code fingerprint. The same day's `days/<day>.json` carries the file's SHA-256 and the forecasts go up as `forecasts/<day>.csv.gz` (Call 7): anyone can keep today's file and check it against the outcomes in two weeks. Forecasts are issued from the 30th day of records at the chain; before that there is too little history for A, let alone the model.
- **F6 Scoring.** On the morning of d+15 the build scores day d's frozen file against the outcomes and writes `scores/date=<d>/scores-<run id>.parquet` once, locked. A forecast whose 14-day window holds more than 4 holes (chain-days with promotion files from fewer than half the chain's included stores; a Shufersal day whose promotion list the collector's check did not read in full is not a hole while its files cover half the stores, since a chain-wide promotion is in every store's file, and the record page lists such days) is unscored and counted; every other forecast scores as (p − outcome)². The Brier score is the mean over a day's scored forecasts, then over days; the skill against each baseline is 1 − (the model's Brier ÷ the baseline's). Intervals come from a block bootstrap over days (resampling days, not forecasts: a chain's items move together, so 39,000 forecasts in a day are far fewer than 39,000 independent trials).
- **F7 Calibration.** Forecasts bucketed by the model's probability into ten bins; for each bin, the mean forecast, the observed share of outcome 1 and the count. The calibration error is the count-weighted mean absolute gap between the two across bins.
- **F8 The gate.** The odds appear on product pages only when all of these hold, on the owner's go, and the record page states the date they were met: (1) at least 90 days of records at the chain and at least 60 scored forecast days (so about day 105 from the first record: forecasts from day 30, the first scored on day 45, 60 scored days by day 104); (2) at least 500 scored forecasts with outcome 1 across the record, so the bins have mass; (3) the model's Brier score below both baselines' over every scored day, with the 95% bootstrap interval of each difference excluding zero; (4) the model's constants fixed before the last 30 scored days (their values are in every frozen file, so this is checkable), so the skill is out of sample; (5) the calibration error at most 0.03, and no bin holding 200 or more forecasts off by more than 0.10. The gate is per chain: a chain whose record falls short shows no odds for its items while the others do. **Closing:** if over the latest 60 scored days the model loses to either baseline, the odds come down for that chain and the record page says so and since when; they return by the same gate.
- **F9 Before the gate.** The product page shows P8's measured history and one sentence: "No forecast yet. The site has scored its own forecasts for this chain privately since 1 December 2026; they go public once they beat two rules of thumb over 60 days (Track record)." (the date illustrative) The track record page shows the scorecard from the first scored day, baselines beside it, with the gate's five conditions and which hold. If the model never beats the baselines, that stays the published result, and the page says the rules of thumb are as good as the site's forecast.
- **F10 How the record is shown.** On the track record page, per month and in all: forecast days scored, forecasts scored and unscored, outcome-1 share, Brier for the model and both baselines, skill with its interval, calibration as a ten-row table (forecast, observed, count) and an inline chart, and one plain sentence per bin that matters ("Of the items the site gave about a 1-in-5 chance, 19% were on promotion within 14 days"). Every model version has its own rows and dates. On a product page, once the gate is open: "Odds of a promotion here within 14 days: about 1 in 6 (this site's forecast; scored on 71 days so far, Track record)", the 1-in-N rounding the flight-board site uses (below 20 to the nearest whole number, from 20 up to the nearest multiple of 5). Never "will", "likely" or "expected to".

## What the site needs from the reader

The promotion reader is a language model that reads a promotion's Hebrew fine print into structured fields, with its accuracy published per field against a hand-checked set. Its design is a separate note; this one fixes what the site consumes, so the two can be built against one contract.

**Input, from the build:** one record per distinct promotion text per chain per day: chain, `PromotionID`, `PromotionDescription`, start and end, `PromotionUpdateTime`, the structured fields of the promotion and its groups and items (P3's fields), the listed prices of its items that day, and the count of stores carrying it. The same promotion appears in hundreds of store files; the reader reads each text once, and a text already read under the same (chain, id, description, start, end) is not read again. How many distinct texts a chain lists a day is UNVERIFIED (258 promotions in one Shufersal store's file; the sample notes that two stores share one catalogue).

**Output, one record per input, a versioned JSON schema (R1):**

| Field | Values | What the site does with it |
|---|---|---|
| `status` | `read`, `unread` (not run, failed, or failed validation), `not_a_shelf_deal` (coupon, gift, voucher) | `unread`: shown with "fine print not read", never ranked; `not_a_shelf_deal`: counted, not shown |
| `kind` | `fixed_price`, `n_for_m`, `percent_off`, `buy_x_get_y`, `gift`, `coupon`, `other` | P4's deal price when the fields do not validate; "5-in-1" is not "5 for 1" (the sample's trap) |
| `unit_price`, `bundle_count`, `bundle_price`, `percent` | numbers, ₪ or percent, or null | P4; a `unit_price` outside (₪0.01, twice the listed price) fails validation |
| `club` | `none`, `chain_club`, `chain_card`, `other`, with the name as written | P5; the club-only filter |
| `min_spend` | ₪ or null | P5; the minimum-spend filter |
| `max_quantity`, `days`, `hours`, `while_stocks_last`, `other_conditions` | as read, or null | P5, shown as conditions |
| per field: `confidence` (0 to 1), `evidence` (the text span), `agrees_with_fields` (true, false, no field) | | a field below the threshold set from the hand-checked set is shown as "unread"; a disagreement with the chain's fields is shown as such, the fields' value kept |
| `category` (later) | one of a short fixed list | the category filter and pooling for F3; its accuracy published like any field |
| `reader_version`, `read_at` | | the accuracy table is per version |

**What the site publishes about the reader (R2):** on the track record page, per field and per version: the hand-checked set's size, date and how it was drawn (at random across chains and deal kinds, labelled before the reader's output was seen), precision and recall for the yes-or-no fields, exact-match rate for the numbers, and the share of texts `unread`. The chain's structured fields are the first answer key where they exist (139 of 142 shelf deals agreed in the sample) and the hand-checked set covers what the fields do not say (club, days, caps). Fields the text never states (`RedemptionLimit`, `MaxQty`, set on 104 and 4 of the sample's promotions) are not graded against the text.

**Order (Call 8):** this design first, fixing the contract; the reader's design next, against it; neither build waits for the other. Step 1 of the site ships on the chains' fields alone, with every promotion labelled "fine print not read"; the reader slots in at step 2 with no page changing shape, only its labels. The reader's cost per morning is the reader design's to estimate; the site's build never calls a model.

## Build and hosting

**Static, yes.** The pages are pure functions of the archive and the day; nothing a visitor does needs a server. What changes from the basket's two pages is scale: about 21,000 to 25,000 product pages (P1), about 25 browse pages, the front page, three fixed pages and a few hundred small data files. Search runs in the browser over an index the build writes: one JSON piece per first letter of the published names and one for barcodes (at about 100 bytes a product, about 2 MB in all, pieces of 20 to 150 KB; CloudFront compresses them: `Compress: true` in `site-template.yaml`), fetched on the first keystroke; Hebrew is matched after stripping quotes and geresh marks and collapsing final letters, and a typed barcode goes straight to its page. Without a script, the browse-by-letter pages reach every product.

**Scripts (Call 2).** The basket's upload check refuses any `<script`; the flight-board site allows its one search script. Here the check allows exactly two files of the build's own, `search.js` and `odds.js`, and refuses any other script tag. `odds.js` exists for cost: once the gate is open, every covered product's odds change every day (the days since its last promotion grow), so rendering them into the HTML means re-uploading every product page daily; instead the build writes today's odds into 256 pieces under `odds/` (about 150 pairs a piece), the product page fetches its piece and fills the odds line, and without a script the line reads "Today's odds for this item: see the track record page" with the measured history above it intact. Product pages then change only when something on them changes (a price, a promotion, the names), which prices do rarely (1.9% of one store's items carried an October `PriceUpdateTime` in the sample, if that field marks a price change: UNVERIFIED) and promotions do about weekly. The alternative, odds in the HTML and a daily re-upload of every page, is about $3 a month more (the cost section) and plain HTML throughout; the owner's call.

**The daily build.** The same scheduled function as the basket (`BasketFunction`, 2,048 MB, 900 s, a DuckDB layer; [operations.md](operations.md), "Sizing"), gated as today on the collector's check record, in two modes after the 11:48 check:

1. **Extract, per chain (12:03, five at once as today):** the full price files into the prices table as now, then the chain's full promotion files into `tables/promotions/chain=<slug>/date=<day>/`: one row per (promotion, group, item code) with the promotion's fields and the count of stores carrying it (store-level links are not kept: a chain's promotions are the same in every store, and the count is what P3 needs), and one row per distinct text for the reader; and `tables/items/chain=<slug>/date=<day>/`: one row per (chain, barcode) with P2's median and range, the stores listing it, and today's chain-wide promotion with P4's deal price and P5's field-based conditions. The account's Lambda quota in il-central-1 is five concurrent runs (recorded in the project's notes on 2026-10-29, outside this repository; UNVERIFIED here), and the flight-board collector shares the account, so promotions ride in the same five runs rather than five more. Shufersal's promotion files are the big ones: 50.2 MB compressed on 2026-10-31 against 111.2 MB of price files, but one store's promotion file unpacked to 10.2 MB with 21,244 item links, 56 times its compressed size, so the chain's day could be about 2.8 GB of XML and 6 million links, most of them two meal-card vouchers (the research sample's estimates). Streamed as the price files are (`basket.pricefile` reads items one at a time), with the two vouchers' links counted and dropped early, an estimate of 2 to 5 minutes for Shufersal beside its 102 s of prices: UNVERIFIED until step one measures it on the Mac, as the basket did.
2. **Site (12:33 today, moved later if the measurement says so):** reads the five chains' items tables for the past 90 days (about 110,000 rows a day, one per GS1 code a chain lists, about 39,000 of them chain-wide; so about ten million rows: seconds in DuckDB), the reader's outputs for the day (step 2 on), the frozen forecasts of d−14 for scoring (F6), writes the day's frozen forecasts (F5), prices the basket as now, renders every page and the index pieces into a work folder, runs the checks, and uploads: pages whose bytes differ from what the bucket holds (the build keeps a manifest of each published file's hash in the bucket and compares against it, so an unchanged page costs no request), the data files, then one invalidation of `/*`. It pings the basket's dead-man check as the basket run does, with the day's summary.

**Checks before upload**, as the basket has `basket.check` and `basket.guard`, extended:

- The words check over every page as written and as read: no NEVER phrase; the credit line, "Not shopping advice." and `noindex` on every page; "we", "our", "us" never; the ALWAYS phrases on the front page and every product page; `<script` only for the two allowed files; while a chain's gate is closed, no odds word ("odds", "chance", "1 in", "forecast") on any product page of that chain (the gate's state is in the day file); every link in the build resolving to a file in the build; a product page at most 60 KB and an index piece at most 150 KB.
- The guard: `days/`, `forecasts/` and `scores/` are append-only in the bucket and a published day changes only as a revision (B12, the existing guard, now over three folders); a frozen forecast file is never replaced; the accuracy tables on the record page equal the day file's.
- The build refuses to publish a day earlier than the site's latest (call 12 of the basket's step two, kept), refuses a day whose item tables are missing for a chain without saying so on the page (P10's sentence), and refuses when any ALWAYS phrase is missing.

**Where the site lives:** the existing stack `il-grocery-prices-site` (private bucket, CloudFront, `noindex`), unchanged but for one lifecycle rule (Call 9): noncurrent versions of `p/`, `browse/`, `search/` and `odds/` expire after 30 days, since the bucket keeps every version (B12's second guard) and product pages are many; `days/`, `forecasts/` and `scores/` keep every version.

## Words

Always: "listed price", "as listed in the chain's price files", "before promotions" (beside every listed price), "listed in the morning's files" (beside every promotion), the date line in every header, the credit line in every footer, the source of every condition ("from the chain's fields", "read from the fine print", "fine print not read"), "this site's forecast" beside every odds figure, "of the N days with records" beside every count over days.

Never: the basket's list ("cheapest chain", "cheaper than", "you'll pay", "saves you", "at the till", "real prices", "the chain charges"), and now "fake", "fake discount", "fake sale", "scam", "rip-off", "trick", "inflated", "misleading", "deceptive", "pretends", "true price", "you save", "best deal", "guaranteed", "will be on sale", "will go on sale", "expected to", "likely to", any sentence that gives a chain a motive, and any odds word on a product page while its chain's gate is closed. The page tests pin both lists; the upload check refuses any NEVER phrase anywhere.

The public title of the browse view is "Deals below the usual listed price" (Call 1): "real deals" says that the rest are not real, and the site has measured no such thing. The owner's three feature names stay the project's names for the features.

## Order of building

1. **Step one, no reader, no forecast.** Promotion extraction (P3, P10) with a measurement of its time and memory on the Mac; the items table; product pages, the browse pages, the front page with chain and minimum-spend filters (the club filter from the fields alone, labelled "from the chains' fields; Shufersal's files mark none"); every promotion labelled "fine print not read"; the method and record pages (the record page with the days, the shortfalls and an empty forecast scorecard); the basket page; search; the frozen forecasts begin privately on day 30 (F5), unscored until day 45 and unpublished until the gate. Ships by hand first (`scripts/publish.sh`, the owner's go), then scheduled, as the basket did.
2. **Step two, the reader.** The reader's outputs join the items table; conditions from the text; the club-only filter trustworthy and labelled by source; the accuracy table on the record page (R2); the category filter if the reader supplies one.
3. **Step three, the odds.** On the owner's go once F8 holds for a chain: `odds.js` and the odds line; the record page states the date.
4. **Later:** the update files collected through the day feed promotions listed for part of a day (a mark, not a new feature); a sub-chain cut; a Hebrew page; a custom domain and indexing at launch.

## How success is judged

For the shopper: a weekly hand check of ten deals, read by eye in three chains' raw files (read-only, never copied), agreeing with the pages; the reader's per-field precision at or above the bar the reader's design sets, published; the forecast's gate met, or the honest "the rules of thumb are as good" published; outsider sessions of the kind the flight-board site planned (a few people asked what the site is and whether they would open it before a shop), with their answers written down. For the portfolio: a hiring reader can say in half a minute what the site does and that its claims are measured; the record page shows frozen forecasts scored over months, with the model's losses as visible as its wins; no NEVER phrase has reached the site (the check's job); every morning since launch has a page, or an email explains why not.

## Testing (the project's standard)

- A test and a named mutation for every rule P1 to P10, F1 to F10, R1 and R2 (the schema's validation and the status rules); coverage floor 95% on the new package.
- Promotion extraction on the trimmed fixtures (`tests/fixtures/*/PromoFull*`) and new synthetic ones for the traps: a coupon, a gift item, a voucher over thousands of items, a `RewardType` 0 link with no price, a weighed "2 for ₪100" stored as 1.5 for 75, a `MinPurchaseAmount` on a second group, a promotion carried by one store, an unknown field (P10 stops), a chain whose `ClubID` is a bare code and one whose is a labelled string.
- A golden 90 days: a synthetic five-chain history of a few products with known promotion runs, holes and a price rise, whose pages, windows, gaps, forecasts, scores and calibration are known by hand; the gate's five conditions each failing alone.
- Determinism: the same archive days give the same pages, frozen files and scores byte for byte, apart from the provenance fields.
- Page tests: the ALWAYS and NEVER lists, the two allowed scripts and no other, the gate-closed odds words refused, every link resolving, page size caps, `noindex`, the credit line, Hebrew names in `<span lang="he" dir="rtl">`.
- Test data never reads the clock. A fresh reviewer who has not seen the reasoning reads the code before it ships; the first upload is by hand on the owner's go; the scheduled modes deploy with their schedules off, one manual run each, then on, on the owner's go.

- **Lambda.** Promotion extraction, estimated 2 to 5 minutes for Shufersal and under a minute for each other chain, about 600 s a day at 2 GB: 1,200 GB-seconds a day, 36,000 a month; the site run (history, forecasts, scoring, 25,000 pages rendered, the changed ones uploaded through a thread pool) estimated at 300 to 500 s at 2 GB: 600 to 1,000 a day, 18,000 to 30,000 a month. About 55,000 to 70,000 GB-seconds a month in all, worst case (every run its full 900 s) about 108,000. The account's free tier is already near its edge once the update files are collected through the day (that design's own estimate: 350,000 to 430,000 a month with the collector, the basket and the flight-board collector), so these may all fall outside it: $0.90 to $1.20 a month expected, $1.80 worst.
- **S3 requests.** Changed pages a day with `odds.js`: a product page changes when its price, promotion or names change; promotions turn over weekly and prices rarely, so an estimate of 2,000 to 5,000 pages a day, plus 256 odds pieces, about 25 index pieces and a few data files: 70,000 to 160,000 writes a month, $0.40 to $0.90, with the hash manifest's reads inside the bound. Without `odds.js`, every product page daily once the gate is open: 25,000 writes a day, 750,000 a month, about $4.10. Every build's ~1,000 reads of the archive (the promotion files) and the tables: under $0.20.
- **S3 storage.** The site, about 25,000 pages at about 12 KB and the pieces: about 300 MB, $0.01 a month; noncurrent versions with the 30-day rule, at 2,000 to 5,000 changed pages a day, 0.7 to 1.8 GB held, $0.02 to $0.05 (without the rule, growing by about $0.02 to $0.05 a month for ever; without `odds.js`, by about $0.23 a month for ever). Derived tables: promotions about 10 to 20 MB a day across the chains (counts, not store links) and items about 2 MB: 0.4 to 0.7 GB a month, $0.01 the first month, about $0.15 a month after a year. Frozen forecasts and scores: about 1 MB a day each, locked ten years: about $0.02 a month after a year. The archive bucket keeps noncurrent table versions as today (headline-basket.md, "Cost").
- **CloudFront, healthchecks.io.** No new check (the basket's pings carry the site run); traffic inside the free tier; one invalidation a day.
- **The reader.** The reader design's to estimate (the model's price per text and the texts a day); the site's build calls no model.

## Calls made in this draft (the owner can overturn any; the first is most his)

1. **The browse view's public title is "Deals below the usual listed price", not "Real deals this week"**; "fake" and its kin join the NEVER list. The feature keeps its name in the project.
2. **Two scripts, `search.js` and `odds.js`, allowed by name; the odds fetched from daily pieces so product pages change only on events.** The alternative, odds in the HTML and a daily re-upload of every page, costs about $3 a month more and trips the budget alarm.
3. **Covered products: GS1 barcodes chain-wide (at least half a chain's included stores) at some chain on some day of the past 90 days**, about 21,000 to 25,000 pages; not the 70,698 listed anywhere, not only promoted products.
4. **Depth is measured against the 30-day median listed price**, with the 30- and 90-day lows and the days at or below the deal price shown beside it.
5. **Chain level, with the store range shown; Shufersal's formats not split**, a later query over the same tables.
6. **The gate's numbers**: 90 days of records and 60 scored days, 500 outcome-1 forecasts, skill over both baselines with a day-bootstrap interval excluding zero, constants fixed before the last 30 scored days, calibration error at most 0.03 and no bin of 200 off by more than 0.10; per chain; a closing rule.
7. **The frozen forecasts are published the same day** as `forecasts/<day>.csv.gz`, with the file's hash in the day file.
8. **This design fixes the reader contract; the reader is designed next against it; step one ships without it.**
9. **A 30-day expiry for noncurrent versions of product, browse, search and odds files** in the site bucket; day files, forecasts and scores keep every version.
10. **The basket page stays, as a page of this site**, its items linked to product pages; its day files go on.
11. **Coupons, gift items and vouchers are counted, never shown as deals.**
12. **English with Hebrew product names, as the basket**; a Hebrew page later.
13. **A product page's names are the chains' published strings, labelled "(as X lists it)"**, the longest as the title; no hand-written names beyond the basket's 32.
14. **Promotions ride in the five extraction runs**, not in five more, for the account's concurrency.
15. **The two meal-card vouchers' links are dropped at extraction**, counted, since they carry most of the links and no shelf deal.
16. **Odds are rounded to "about 1 in N"** as the flight-board site rounds them.
