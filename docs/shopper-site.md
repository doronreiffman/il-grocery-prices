## What this piece is, and why

The collector keeps, every morning, the price files and promotion files five Israeli chains publish under the law ([collector.md](collector.md)). The headline basket turned one morning's price files into one number. This site turns the whole archive into something a shopper acts on: for one product at one chain, whether today's promotion is a price the item has not been listed at lately or one it is listed at most weeks anyway; how often the item goes on promotion and how long since the last one; and, once the site has earned it, the odds of a promotion in the next two weeks, with the site's own record of how right those odds have been. The portfolio point is the basket's, carried further: data engineering on messy public data, claims no stronger than the evidence, and a forecast whose accuracy is published before anyone is asked to trust it, as the owner's flight-board site does for a traveller's own flight.

Two readers: a shopper deciding today, who finds one product and reads one page; and a hiring reader skimming the portfolio, who should see in half a minute what the site does, that its claims are measured, and who built it.

Others already compare current prices across chains (CHP, Pricez, Salai, Smoody) and that is fine: novelty is not a filter (owner, 2026-11-05). None of them, as far as a search of 2026-10-31 found (UNVERIFIED in this revision), publishes how a promotion price compares with the item's own listed history, or a scored forecast.

## What a visitor sees

One site in English with Hebrew product names, as the basket page is ([headline-basket.md](headline-basket.md), call 2); a Hebrew version is a later step (Q7).

1. **The front page** (`index.html`): "Deals below their recent listed prices" (Q4), with the rule under it ("promotion prices lower than every listed price of the item in the same store format over the past 90 days with records", P7, Q3). Under the site's name, the date line: "from the chains' price and promotion files of Friday 6 November 2026". Then: a count line ("Today the five chains list 1,840 promotions across a store format on 6,210 products; 410 of them are below every listed price of the past 90 days with records in their store format, on items with at least 20 days of records there in the past 30"; figures illustrative); a search box (a name or a barcode); the table of those deals sorted by the depth they show, a whole percent, deepest first, then by the exact depth, filterable by chain, club-only and minimum spend (later by the reader's category); one deal's story as a paragraph in the product page's words; the line for the hiring reader ("Built by …, How this is made, Track record, Code on GitHub"); the credit line.
2. **A product page** (`p/<barcode>.html`), one per product the site covers (P1): the names each chain lists, as published ("Grissini classic 200 g (as Shufersal lists it)"), the barcode and the size the chains state; then one section per chain that lists it: today's listed price and the range across its stores; today's promotion, if any, with its price, its conditions and where each came from (the chain's fields, the reader, or "fine print not read"); **"The deal against its listed prices"** (the project's "Is this deal real?"), in P7's sentences; **"How often it goes on promotion"** (the project's "Wait for the sale?"): P8's measured history and, once that chain's gate is open (F9), the odds of a promotion within 14 days with the record's one-line summary; the listed-price history as text and a small inline chart (the days on promotion marked). Then a row across chains: today's listed price at each chain, with no ranking words.
3. **Browse-by-letter pages** (`browse/<letter>.html`): every covered product by the first letter of its longest published name, plus one page for codes with no Hebrew name; the path to every product without a script.
4. **"How this is made"** (`method.html`): the rules below in plain words, the words the site never uses, what is measured and what is inferred, the law, how anyone with the chains' files reproduces a page.
5. **"Track record"** (`record.html`): the forecast scorecard (F11) from the first scored day, baselines beside it, each chain's gate open or closed and why; links to every day's forecasts and scores files and to the ledger; the reader's accuracy per field (R2); the days the site has, and the days a chain's files were short.
6. **The basket page** (`basket.html`): the headline basket as it is today, its 32 items linked to their product pages; its day files go on accruing unchanged (Call 2).
7. **Data files**: `site-days/<day>.json` (the day's counts, every number on the front page, each chain's gate state, the day's forecasts hash; Call 8), `forecasts/<day>.csv.gz` and `forecasts/ledger.csv` (F6), `scores/<day>.csv.gz` (F7), `search/<piece>.json`. The basket's `days/<day>.json` stays as it is.

Every page: the header band with the site's name and the date line, `noindex` until the owner launches, the credit line in the footer ("Prices and promotions from the price and promotion files Shufersal, Rami Levy, Osher Ad, Carrefour and Yochananof publish under section 30 of the Food and Pharma Competition Promotion Law, 2014: the files of 6 November 2026, collected by 12:03 Israel time. Listed prices and listed promotions, not a promise of what a till charges. Not shopping advice."), the look of the basket page (one style block, system fonts, nothing loaded from elsewhere, dark mode, phones). The date line, the credit line, P8's "days since", the window counts and the store counts change every morning, so every page is rebuilt and uploaded every morning (Q2).

## Goals

- A shopper answers "take this deal, or wait?" for one product at one chain in under a minute, from measured history and plainly labelled inferences.
- Every number reproducible: the rules published, each day's inputs named in the site-day file, the same code on the same files giving the same page; every forecast score recomputable from the published forecasts and scores files.
- Odds shown to shoppers only after beating two rules of thumb on forecasts provably frozen before their outcomes; every frozen forecast and score public from the first day (Q1).
- A fresh site every morning without the owner, from the files the collector stored that day; the pages say which day they show.
- Cheap: about $4 to $6 a month on top of the collector in year one, or about $0.60 to $2.50 with Q2's alternative (the cost section).

## Non-goals (version one)

- Prices or promotions by store, sub-chain or city: chain level, with the store range shown, as the basket does (Call 1; Shufersal's formats price differently, P2).
- Promotions that start and end within a day, or appear only in update files: the update-files design collects those through the day, but this site reads the morning's full files only until a later step, and says "listed in the morning's files".
- Coupons, gift items and meal-card vouchers: counted, never shown as deals (P3, Call 3).
- Delivered prices, online stores, unit-price comparisons across different sizes, brand or private-label analysis, a food index.
- Product matching by name: a product is a barcode; produce and other internal codes get no page.
- A server, a database behind the pages, accounts, alerts to shoppers.
- Airflow and dbt: the build is a handful of steps in one scheduled function, the rules SQL over Parquet, as the basket's are; an orchestrator arrives when the step count earns it.

## Anti-goals (a failure even if everything else works)

- A claim the files cannot back: a motive ("the chain inflates its prices before a sale"), a till price, a saving "for you", a store named as dear or cheap. The site measures gaps and reports them.
- The word "fake", or any word that says a chain deceives. The Consumer Protection Authority's spokesman warned that manipulative use of the data invites misleading-claims suits (Globes via N12, 2025-12-16, a secondary source, as [terms.md](terms.md) cites it). The measured gap, in the chain's own listed prices, is the whole statement.
- Odds shown to a shopper before the gate opens, or a record that resets quietly when the model changes. Every frozen forecast names its model version; the record page shows every version's days.
- A page that looks current but is not: today's date in every header, so a page the build missed shows its age; the morning dead-man check; pages always show the site's latest day (Call 9).
- A number nobody can reproduce, or one that changes quietly: B12's rule holds for day files and accuracy tables; frozen forecasts and scores are never changed at all.
- Raw files or per-store price lists published; anything that touches the portals (the build reads the archive only).
- A page whose measured facts need a script: the one script, search, only finds pages (Q2).

## Definitions and rules

Each rule has a name, tests of its own and a mutation in `scripts/mutation_check.py`. Chain-level price rules reuse the basket's where they apply (B1 files, B2 included stores, B3 rows, B4 placeholders, B5 the chain's median).

- **P1 A product.** A barcode that passes the GS1 check digit, leading zeros stripped, as the basket defines it (headline-basket.md, "The basket"); internal codes (weighed produce, chain codes) are not products here. The site covers a product when, on some day of the past 90 days, at least half of some chain's included stores (B2) listed it (B6's "chain-wide" test); once covered, a product keeps its page, rebuilt every morning and saying when it was last seen listed across each store format (Call 10), except on a morning that is a hole for that format, whose few files cannot tell whether the product is listed across it that day. Within one chain a product is (chain, barcode), across chains the same barcode. Measured on the five prices tables of 2026-11-02: 70,698 distinct GS1 barcodes priced above ₪0.01 at any store (Shufersal 38,064; Rami Levy 25,879; Carrefour 20,768; Yochananof 16,902; Osher Ad 9,659); 21,324 listed by at least half of some chain's stores with every store counted, 8,487 of them at two or more chains; 21,973 with B2's exclusions applied by file name. So about 22,000 product pages on one day, more over 90 days (Q5).
- **P2 Listed price.** A chain's listed price for a product on a day is rule B5: the median of the product's price across the chain's included stores whose latest full price file that day lists it, placeholders dropped (B4); the lowest and highest store prices are kept and shown. A chain-day with files from fewer than half its included stores (B8) is a hole: no listed price that day, never a zero. Shufersal's formats price differently (one Sheli store was dearer than one Deal store on 89% of shared items, median 7.7%, on two stores' files of 2026-10-31: UNVERIFIED in this revision), so the chain's median blurs them; the range beside every price shows the spread (Call 1).
- **P3 A promotion.** A `<Promotion>` in a chain's full promotion files (`PromoFull`) of the day, linked to the product's barcode through its groups' items, whose start is on or before the day and whose end is on or after it. The stores counted are P2's, the chain's included stores with a price file that counts that day (Call 7): B2 reaches promotion files through the store id only, since Shufersal's are named under sub-chain 000 as far as seen ([portals.md](portals.md); the fixture `PromoFull7290027600007-000-878-…`), so the file-name half of B2 (`src/basket/rules.py`) cannot apply, while the store-list half (`src/basket/stores.py`, `included`) still leaves Be's 97 stores and store 413 out. Excluded and counted: coupons (`AdditionalIsCoupon` = 1), gift items, and any promotion whose fields give no price effect (a `RewardType` 0 link with no `DiscountedPrice` or `DiscountRate`). How each chain marks a gift item is UNVERIFIED: Shufersal's fixture leaves `IsGiftItem` empty, but the fixtures keep the real files' fields bar prices, names and barcodes ([collector-plan.md](collector-plan.md), "Global Constraints") and hold `IsGiftItem` 1 and 3 at Carrefour and 6, 2 and 2 at Rami Levy (equal to those promotions' `MinQty`), so "`IsGiftItem` set" is not the gift test until step one reads each chain's values. In one Shufersal store's file of 2026-10-31 (the research sample, UNVERIFIED in this revision), 116 of 258 promotions were coupons, two meal-card vouchers among them carrying about three quarters of all item links. A promotion is **listed chain-wide** when the promotion files of at least half the counted stores carry it; one carried by fewer is counted, shown as "at some stores" and never ranked. On a morning that is a hole for a format (P2, under "Changed after the survey"), every promotion of that format is shown "at some stores", with none of P7's facts, no deal in P9's row and no date a promotion is listed to start, since its few files cannot tell whether a promotion is listed across it (deploy prep, 2026-11-10). The fields' meanings (`RewardType` 2 percent off, 3 fixed price, 1 and 10 "N for ₪M" with `MinQty` N and `DiscountedPrice` M; `MinPurchaseAmount` on a second group for "only with a ₪X basket") were read from Shufersal's data, not from a specification: UNVERIFIED for the other four chains until the first extraction compares them (the build stops loudly on a field set it does not know: P10).
- **P4 Deal price.** The price per unit after the promotion, when buying the least that qualifies: a fixed price is `DiscountedPrice`; percent off is the listed price × (1 − `DiscountRate`/100), with `DiscountedPrice` as a check; "N for ₪M" is M ÷ N, shown with "buy N" as a condition. The fields validate when they agree with each other and the deal price is above ₪0.01 and below twice the listed price; 1,478 of 1,482 non-coupon item links agreed with the shelf price in the sample. A promotion whose fields do not validate takes the reader's reading (R1) when it has one, else it is **unread**: listed with "price not read", never ranked. Weighed goods are the known trap: the sample's three disagreements were all meat ("2 for ₪100" stored as 1.5 for ₪75, the same per-kilogram rate), so a deal price per kilogram is shown per kilogram.
- **P5 Conditions.** Club-only: the promotion's target field says club (the regulations' schedule 3 codes, as read from the Hebrew Wikisource copy of the price-transparency regulations on 2026-10-31: 0 none, 1 club, 2 the chain's credit card, 3 other; the official text is unread: UNVERIFIED) or the reader found a club or card restriction in the text (Shufersal's field read "all customers" on every one of the sample's 258, and its "תו זהב" and meal-card restrictions appeared only in the text). Minimum spend: `MinPurchaseAmount` on a group, or the reader's reading of "מותנה N" and its kin. From the reader also: a quantity cap, a day or hour restriction, "while stocks last". Each condition names its source; a promotion with no reading yet carries "fine print not read: club-only or other conditions may apply".
- **P6 Windows.** The past 30 and 90 days are calendar days ending the day before the page's day, counting only days with a listed price at that chain (holes skipped, never filled). A window with fewer than 20 of 30 or 60 of 90 days of records says so ("23 days of records since 1 November 2026"), and every count is "of the N days with records".
- **P7 The gap (the project's "Is this deal real?").** Three measured facts, in this order, each a sentence: the deal price against the 30-day median listed price (the reference for depth), as money and a whole percent ("₪4.00, 34%, below the 30-day median listed price of ₪11.90"; "under 1%" for a gap above ₪0 that rounds to 0%); the deal price against the lowest listed price of the past 30 and 90 days, each said over the days of records its window holds, and on how many of the 90's days with records the item was listed at or below the deal price without a promotion ("The lowest listed price was ₪11.90 in the 30 days before today and ₪10.90 in the 90 days before today; listed at ₪7.90 or less on 0 of the 90 days with records"); and any change in the listed price in the past 30 days ("the listed price rose from ₪10.90 to ₪11.90 on 28 October"). Depth is (30-day median listed price − deal price) ÷ that median. No verdict word: the reader of the page draws the conclusion. **The front page's test (Q3):** a deal is listed there when it is listed chain-wide, its deal price validates (P4), the item has at least 20 days of records in the past 30 at that chain (deploy prep, 2026-11-10: so the 30-day median its row shows rests on 20 days or more, P6's line for a window that is not short), and the deal price is below every listed price of the past 90 days with records. Nearly every promotion is below the 30-day median of shelf prices; a listed price raised shortly before the promotion cannot move the 90-day low.
- **P8 Promotion history (the project's "Wait for the sale?", the measured part).** On how many of the days with records the product was on a chain-wide promotion at that chain; each promotion seen as a date range (consecutive listed days; a hole inside a run does not break it); the gaps between runs, in days; the last run's end and the days since. All from the morning files.
- **P9 Across chains.** One row: each chain's listed price today and its deal price today if any, with the conditions' presence marked; sorted by chain name, never by price; no "cheaper", no rank.
- **P10 Fail loud on a format change.** A promotion file whose field set differs from the one the fixtures hold, or a `RewardType`, target or gift code outside the set seen, stops that chain's extraction with an error naming the field, and the day's build goes on without that chain's promotions and says so on the page ("Promotions from Carrefour's files could not be read today"). The Authority is rewriting the promotion format with a uniform definition of promotions (Globes via N12, 2025-12-16, secondary); the first sign of it must be an email, not a quiet zero.

**Measured or inferred.** Measured, from the files by rule: listed prices and ranges, promotion presence and dates, deal prices where the fields validate, field-based conditions, the gaps and counts of P7 and P8. Computed by rule and named as such: the chain's median, chain-wide coverage, depth. Inferred, and labelled on the page: the reader's readings (R1), with their confidence; the forecast (F). Nothing on a page mixes the two without saying which is which.

## The forecast (the project's "Wait for the sale?", the odds)

A careful forecaster freezes each forecast before its outcome, where others can keep a copy; scores it once the outcome is known; compares it with rules of thumb scored on the same items; and shows the odds only once it beats them on days it was not fitted to. The rules:

- **F1 Target, version 1.** For a product at a chain on day d: 1 if a chain-wide promotion (P3, by the chains' fields as step one reads them: coupons, gift items and no-price links out; club-only and "price not read" in; "at some stores" out) is listed in the morning full files of at least one of the days d+1 to d+14, else 0. Every forecast file names the target's version; no later reading changes a past outcome, and a new definition is a new model version (Call 13). The outcome is known once day d+14's tables exist (Call 14).
- **F2 Who gets a forecast.** Every covered (chain, product) listed chain-wide on day d and not on a chain-wide promotion that day (an item on promotion today has no "wait" question). If step one finds promotions listed before their start date, an item with one listed to start within d+1 to d+14 gets no forecast: its page says "listed to start on <date>" (measured), and it is counted, not scored (Call 22). About 39,000 to 41,000 (chain, product) pairs were chain-wide on 2026-11-02 (39,324 with every store counted, 40,869 with B2 applied by file name): an upper bound on a day's forecasts.
- **F3 Inputs pinned to day d.** A forecast for day d reads only tables of days up to d, and learns only from past forecast days t whose window has closed by then (t ≤ d − 14, so the outcome is in day d's tables). So do both baselines and every count beside them; a test builds day d with and without later days in the archive and expects the same bytes.
- **F4 Model, version 1: simple first.** A pooled hazard with shrinkage: for each chain, the share of outcome 1 over past closed eligible days among pairs with the same days since their last promotion run ended (0 to 60, and "no promotion seen"), blended with the pair's own share (baseline A, F5) by weight n ÷ (n + n0), n the pair's closed eligible days and n0 one constant fixed in the code before the version's first frozen day. Its outcomes come from the tables (F3), not from scored forecasts, so the history exists from the first record. Anything more (weekday and holiday effects, a category from the reader, a recent change in the listed price) joins only as a new version, named in advance and scored on days after its release.
- **F5 Two baselines, given the same selection.** (A) **The item's base rate:** the pair's share of outcome 1 over its closed eligible days of the past 90 (F3); with fewer than 20 such days, the chain's share. The model is A blended with the hazard, so its skill over A is what the hazard adds. (B) **"The last gap repeats":** with two or more promotion runs seen, the next run starts at the last run's end plus the gap between the last two runs; B says yes if that date falls in d+1 to d+14, else no (no with fewer than two runs). A bare yes or no is punished harder by the Brier score than any probability, so B is scored as the chain's share of outcome 1 over its closed past days on which B gave the same answer, its raw error rate shown beside it (Call 18). Both are frozen beside the model's probability and scored on exactly the forecasts the model is scored on.
- **F6 The daily freeze.** The build of day d writes the day's forecasts once: `forecasts/date=<d>/forecasts.csv.gz` in the archive, write-once and locked like a manifest (`If-None-Match: *`, the ten-year governance lock), and the same bytes on the site as `forecasts/<d>.csv.gz` (rows sorted, gzip without a timestamp, so the bytes are reproducible). One key per day: a rerun compares its bytes with the stored file and records "same" or "differs" (which fails loudly), so there is never a second set to choose from (Call 15). Each row: chain, barcode, the model's probability, A, B, the features used; the file names the model and target versions, n0 and the code fingerprint. A day counts only when frozen on time: the archive copy's time, set by S3 and not by the writer (UNVERIFIED here), before midnight ending day d, Israel time, about when the chains' next files appear (Rami Levy's fixtures are files of 1 November stamped 00:10). The build refuses forecasts for a day past its cutoff: a backfill or late rerun writes none, and the day is a shown gap (Call 16). The ledger, `forecasts/ledger.csv` on the site, gains a line per frozen day (the day, the file's SHA-256, its S3 time) and only grows: the upload check refuses a ledger that does not begin with the published one's bytes, so anyone who kept an earlier copy can see a changed line (Call 17). Forecasts begin on the 30th day of records at the chain; the record starts on the first day frozen on time, never earlier.
- **F7 Scoring.** The build of day d+14 scores day d's frozen file against the outcomes (F1) and writes `scores/date=<d>/scores.csv.gz` once, locked, and the same bytes on the site as `scores/<d>.csv.gz`: each forecast's row with its outcome and the evidence (the first day in the window a chain-wide promotion was listed, and its `PromotionID`), or why it is unscored (Call 21). A forecast whose window holds more than 4 holes (chain-days with promotion files from fewer than half the counted stores; a Shufersal day whose listing the collector did not read in full is not a hole while its files cover half the stores, which assumes a chain's promotions are the same in its stores, as step one measures) is unscored and counted, by a rule fixed in advance and the same for all three. Every other forecast scores (p − outcome)² for the model and each baseline. The Brier score is the mean over a day's scored forecasts, then over days; the skill against a baseline is 1 − (the model's Brier ÷ the baseline's). A missed scoring is done by the next build: scoring needs no clock.
- **F8 Intervals and calibration.** Intervals come from a moving-block bootstrap over scored forecast days: blocks of 14 consecutive days, since consecutive days' windows share up to 13 of their 14 outcome days and a chain's items move together (about 39,000 forecasts a day are far fewer independent trials; 90 scored days hold about six independent fortnights); 10,000 draws; the seed, block length and draw count fixed in the code and printed on the record page (Call 20). Calibration: forecasts bucketed by the model's probability into ten bins of width 0.1; per bin the mean forecast, the observed share of outcome 1 and the count; the calibration error is the count-weighted mean absolute gap. Anyone with the published forecasts and scores files and the code on GitHub gets the same tables and intervals.
- **F9 The gate (Q6).** The odds appear on a chain's product pages only when all of these hold for that chain, on the owner's go, and the record page states the date they were met: (1) at least 90 scored forecast days of the model version in force, each frozen on time (forecasts start on day 30 of records and day d is scored on day d+14, so the earliest opening is day 133); (2) the model's Brier score below both baselines' over those days, with the 95% interval of each difference excluding zero; (3) the calibration error at most 0.03, and no bin holding 1% or more of the scored forecasts off by more than 0.10. Out of sample by construction: version 1 fixes its one constant before its first frozen day and learns the rest from closed windows only (F3); a new version (new constants or target included) starts its count from zero; every version's days stay on the record page with the number of versions tried; a test pins the golden forecasts to the version name, so a change that moves a forecast without a new version fails (Call 19). **Closing:** if over the latest 60 scored days the model's Brier is above either baseline's, the odds come down for that chain, the record page says so and since when, and they return only by the gate; this also catches a chain that passed by luck, which five chains tested at once make likelier.
- **F10 Before the gate.** A chain's section on a product page shows P8's measured history and one fixed sentence: "No odds yet for Shufersal. Every day this site freezes a forecast for each item and scores it two weeks later; the odds appear here once they beat two rules of thumb over 90 days (Track record)." The record page shows the scorecard from the first scored day, the gate's conditions and which hold, and the files, labelled as test records, not advice (Q1). If the model never beats the baselines, that stays the published result: the rules of thumb are as good as the site's forecast.
- **F11 How the record is shown.** On the record page, per chain, per month and in all: days frozen, missed and scored; forecasts scored and unscored; the outcome-1 share; Brier for the model and both baselines; skill with its interval; calibration as a ten-row table and an inline chart, and one plain sentence per bin that matters ("Of the items the site gave about a 1-in-5 chance, 19% were on promotion within 14 days"). Every model version has its own rows. On a product page, once the chain's gate is open: "Odds of a promotion here within 14 days, club-only ones included: about 1 in 6 (this site's forecast; scored on 71 days so far, Track record)", rounded as the flight-board site rounds (below 20 to the nearest whole number, from 20 up to the nearest multiple of 5; Call 24). Never "will", "likely" or "expected to".

## What the site needs from the reader

The promotion reader is a language model that reads a promotion's Hebrew fine print into structured fields, its accuracy published per field against a hand-checked set. Its design is a separate note; this one fixes what the site consumes (Q9).

**Input, from the build:** one record per distinct promotion text per chain per day: chain, `PromotionID`, `PromotionDescription`, start and end, `PromotionUpdateTime`, P3's fields of the promotion and its groups and items, its items' listed prices that day, and the count of stores carrying it. Each text is read once; one already read under the same (chain, id, description, start, end) is not read again. How many distinct texts a chain lists a day is UNVERIFIED (step one measures it).

**Output, one record per input, a versioned JSON schema (R1):**

| Field | Values | What the site does with it |
|---|---|---|
| `status` | `read`, `unread` (not run, failed, or failed validation), `not_a_shelf_deal` (coupon, gift, voucher) | `unread`: shown with "fine print not read", never ranked; `not_a_shelf_deal`: counted, not shown |
| `kind` | `fixed_price`, `n_for_m`, `percent_off`, `buy_x_get_y`, `gift`, `coupon`, `other` | P4's deal price when the fields do not validate; "5-in-1" is not "5 for 1" (the sample's trap) |
| `unit_price`, `bundle_count`, `bundle_price`, `percent` | numbers, ₪ or percent, or null | P4; a `unit_price` outside (₪0.01, twice the listed price) fails validation |
| `club` | `none`, `chain_club`, `chain_card`, `other`, with the name as written | P5; the club-only filter |
| `min_spend` | ₪ or null | P5; the minimum-spend filter |
| `max_quantity`, `days`, `hours`, `while_stocks_last`, `other_conditions` | as read, or null | P5, shown as conditions |
| per field: `confidence` (0 to 1), `evidence` (the text span), `agrees_with_fields` (true, false, no field) | | a field below the threshold set from the hand-checked set is shown as "unread"; a disagreement with the chain's fields is shown as such, the fields' value kept |
| `category` (later) | one of a short fixed list | the category filter, and a candidate feature for a later model version (F4); its accuracy published like any field |
| `reader_version`, `read_at` | | the accuracy table is per version |

**What the site publishes about the reader (R2):** on the record page, per field and version: the hand-checked set's size, date and how it was drawn (at random across chains and deal kinds, labelled before the reader's output was seen), precision and recall for yes-or-no fields, exact-match rate for numbers, and the share `unread`. The chain's structured fields are the first answer key where they exist (139 of 142 shelf deals agreed in the sample) and the hand-checked set covers what the fields do not say (club, days, caps). Fields the text never states (`RedemptionLimit`, `MaxQty`, set on 104 and 4 of the sample's promotions) are not graded against the text. No reading ever changes a forecast's target (F1).

The reader's cost per morning is the reader design's to estimate; the site's build never calls a model.

## Build and hosting

**Static, yes.** The pages are pure functions of the archive and the day; nothing a visitor does needs a server. What changes from the basket's two pages is scale: about 22,000 to 25,000 product pages (P1), about 25 browse pages, four fixed pages and the data files. Search runs in the browser over an index the build writes: one JSON piece per first letter of the published names (about 100 bytes a product; a letter over 150 KB splits by its second letter; CloudFront compresses them, `Compress: true` in `site-template.yaml`), fetched on the first keystroke; Hebrew is matched after stripping quotes and geresh marks and collapsing final letters. A typed barcode needs no index: the script asks for `p/<barcode>.html` and, on any answer but 200, says the site has no page for it (S3 answers a missing key with 403 to a reader not allowed to list the bucket, as `template.yaml`'s `ListArchive` comment records, and the site's bucket policy lets CloudFront read only; Call 12). Without a script, the browse pages reach every product.

**One script (Q2).** The upload check allows exactly `search.js` and refuses any other script tag. The odds, at step three, go into the HTML, since every page goes up every morning.

**The daily build.** The basket's scheduled function (`BasketFunction`, 2,048 MB, 900 s, a DuckDB layer; [operations.md](operations.md), "Sizing"), gated as today on the collector's 11:48 check record, in its two modes (Call 5):

1. **Extract, per chain (12:03, five at once as today):** the full price files into the prices table as now; then the chain's full promotion files into `tables/promotions/chain=<slug>/date=<day>/`: one row per (promotion, group, item code) with the promotion's fields and the count of counted stores carrying it (store-level links are not kept), and one row per distinct text for the reader; and `tables/items/chain=<slug>/date=<day>/`: one row per (chain, barcode) with P2's median and range, the stores listing it, and today's chain-wide promotion with P4's deal price and P5's field-based conditions. `basket.pricefile` decompresses a whole file and returns every item (`src/basket/pricefile.py`, `read_items`), so promotions get a reader of their own that yields links one at a time, the two meal-card vouchers' links counted and dropped as they are read (Call 6). The account's Lambda quota in il-central-1 is five concurrent runs (UNVERIFIED here), shared with the flight-board collector, so promotions ride in the same five runs. Shufersal's promotion files are the big ones: 50.2 MB compressed on 2026-10-31 against 111.2 MB of price files, and one store's unpacked to 10.2 MB with 21,244 item links, so the chain's day could be about 2.8 GB of XML and 6 million links, most of them the vouchers' (the research sample's estimates, UNVERIFIED in this revision); 2 to 5 minutes beside its 102 s of prices in Lambda is a guess until step one measures it.

A same-day rerun after a failed upload publishes the archive's forecast bytes. It pings the basket's dead-man check as the basket run does, with the day's summary.

## Order of building

1. **Step one, no reader, no odds.** Promotion extraction (P3, P10) and its measurements (below); the items table; product pages, the browse pages, the front page with chain and minimum-spend filters (the club filter from the fields alone, labelled "from the chains' fields; Shufersal's files mark none"); every promotion labelled "fine print not read"; the method and record pages; the basket page; search; the daily freeze from the 30th day of records, each day's forecasts and ledger line public the same day (Q1), scored from 14 days later; no odds on any product page. 2. **Step two, the reader.** The reader's outputs join the items table; conditions from the text; the club-only filter trustworthy and labelled by source; the accuracy table on the record page (R2); the category filter if the reader supplies one.
3. **Step three, the odds.** On the owner's go once F9 holds for a chain: the odds line written into that chain's sections; the record page states the date.
4. **Later:** the update files feed promotions listed for part of a day (a mark, and a new target version); a sub-chain cut; a Hebrew page; a custom domain and indexing at launch.

## How success is judged

For the shopper: a weekly hand check of ten deals, read by eye in three chains' raw files (read-only, never copied), agreeing with the pages; the reader's per-field precision at or above the bar the reader's design sets, published; the forecast's gate met, or the honest "the rules of thumb are as good" published; a few outsiders asked what the site is and whether they would open it before a shop, their answers written down. For the portfolio: a hiring reader can say in half a minute what the site does and that its claims are measured; the record page shows forecasts frozen in public and scored over months, with the model's losses as visible as its wins; no NEVER phrase has reached the site; every morning since launch has a page, or an email explains why not.

## Testing (the project's standard)

- A test and a named mutation for every rule P1 to P10, F1 to F11, R1 and R2 (the schema's validation and the status rules); coverage floor 95% on the new package.
